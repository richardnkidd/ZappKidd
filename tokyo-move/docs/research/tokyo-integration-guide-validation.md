# Validation log for the Tokyo integration guide

Companion to [tokyo-integration-guide.md](tokyo-integration-guide.md). It records what was checked, against what,
and with what result, so that a later reader can see why each statement in the guide reads the way it does.

## Method

- **Inputs.** Two reports: the long original (*Integrating Into Tokyo: An Evidence-Based Guide for Americans Moving
  to Japan*, 237 web citations) and a review of it dated 21 November 2026 (*Tokyo Integration Guide: QA and
  Revision*, 51 sources).
- **What was checked.** Every legal, administrative, statistical and attributed claim that could change a decision
  or a budget, from both inputs, plus the topics neither covered. Cultural generalisations were not "verified"; they
  were either tied to a named author's actual words or relabelled as judgment.
- **How.** Five independent checking passes on 22 November 2026, one per subject area, each instructed to open the
  primary source, quote the figure, and say plainly when a page could not be opened. Results were then reconciled
  against both inputs by hand.
- **Verdicts.** *Confirmed*: the source says what the claim says. *Corrected*: the source supports a different
  figure, date, scope or attribution. *Contradicted*: the source says otherwise. *Unverified*: no adequate source
  could be opened.

## Limits

- This is a point-in-time check. Rules dated 2026 change; the guide lists the known re-check dates.
- Several official sites refused automated page fetches. Where that happened the page was retrieved another way and
  read, or the row says "search extract only".
- A few sources are in Japanese only and were read in Japanese; the guide paraphrases them.
- Three statements are inferences from a confirmed rule, not statements found in a source. They are marked
  *inferred* below and in the guide.
- One statement is an interpretation of the revised permanent-residence guideline that has not been confirmed by the
  Immigration Services Agency. It is marked *unconfirmed reading* and should go to a lawyer.
- The original report's 237 citations were not individually audited. The review's finding that they were largely
  blogs and forum threads attached to legal and statistical claims was consistent with every one sampled.

## What the review got right, and where it fell short

The review's central judgment was sound: the original was not fit to circulate as evidence-based. Its corrections
on housing law, the two-card advice, the medical-cost ceiling, the status of the permanent-residence guideline, fee
terminology, bicycle enforcement, the Meguro example, the survey denominators and the cultural overclaiming were all
borne out.

Its weaknesses were of a different kind:

1. **It corrected by subtraction.** Faced with a doubtful specific, it usually replaced it with "check locally".
   That is safe and leaves the reader with less than they need. Most of the removed specifics turned out to be
   checkable.
2. **It missed the largest practical items.** Nothing on pets, on Japanese tax residency, on the timing of
   residence tax, or on the detailed permanent-residence factors that now depend on the visa category.
3. **It carried a few errors of its own:** the breadth of the medicines online trial, the move-in cost range, and
   two dead source links.
4. **It did not catch one misread chart** (the 1.7% figure) and did not notice that a figure the original sourced
   to a blog (19.4%) was in fact the correct newer official number.
