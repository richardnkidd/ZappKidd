## 1. Ten findings that change the plan

1. **The dog sets the critical path.** A dog from the continental US needs a microchip, two rabies vaccinations, a
   passing antibody test and then a **180-day wait** before it may land. The realistic minimum is about seven months
   from the first step; plan on eight or more. Miss a step and the alternative is up to 180 days in quarantine.
   **V** [AQS-dog]
2. **The visa category decides more than the right to work.** It affects how the years count toward permanent
   residence, whether a five-year period of stay is reachable, and whether two significant tax shelters apply. Settle
   it with an immigration lawyer and a cross-border tax adviser before the offer is signed, not after. See
   [3.1](#31-status-of-residence-and-employment) and [3.2](#32-money-in-two-countries).
3. **Medicines are judged by active ingredient, not by US legality.** Adderall and Dexedrine cannot enter Japan at
   all. Vyvanse needs permission applied for at least 14 days ahead. Concerta is allowed within a quantity limit.
   **V** [NCD]
4. **Driving depends on which US state issued the licence.** Seven states convert without either test. Everyone else
   faces a 50-question exam at a 90% pass mark and a practical test that press reports say about one applicant in
   eight passes. The international permit runs out one year after landing and cannot be reset by a trip abroad.
   **V** / **S** [TMPD-conversion] [TMPD-idp]
5. **For the first five years, foreign income that stays abroad is generally outside Japanese income tax.** That is
   the non-permanent-resident rule, and it is the single most valuable tax fact for anyone with US rental or
   investment income. It ends on remittance to Japan. **V** [NTA-residency]
6. **Housing costs more up front than the original report said, and the dog shrinks the market.** The transport
   ministry's own guide puts initial costs at four to seven months' rent, not three to five. Roughly one rental
   listing in five nationally accepts pets. **V** [MLIT-guide] / **S** [LIFULL-pets]
7. **Two fourteen-day deadlines start the clock:** register the address, and settle health insurance. A third trap
   comes later: the My Number function of either card type lapses on the original period-of-stay date during a
   pending renewal, which can freeze health-insurance use and bank accounts. **V** [ISA-faq] [ISA-special-period]
8. **Immigration fees rose sharply on 1 October 2026 and are charged per person, per permission.** A three-year
   renewal is now ¥64,000 at the counter (¥56,000 online); permanent residence is ¥200,000 on approval. **V**
   [ISA-fees]
9. **The everyday rules are local, specific and checkable.** Rubbish goes out by 8:00 on collection morning in
   Meguro and Setagaya, in an ordinary see-through bag. Cycling fines run from ¥3,000 to ¥12,000. Riding after
   drinking is a crime, not a fine. **V** [Meguro-waste] [NPA-rulebook]
10. **The cultural advice in the original was overconfident; the opinion data was mostly right but misread.**
    Public caution about immigration has risen steeply and measurably. It is also selective, and none of it predicts
    how one household will be treated. Treat cultural frameworks as questions to ask, not rules to obey.

## 3. Before the move

### 3.1 Status of residence and employment

Have the employer or an immigration adviser confirm, in writing, the permitted activities, the application route, the
start date and who handles renewals. Include any work done remotely for an overseas employer. Assess each adult's
status separately: permission for one person does not settle the other's right to work. **J**

Questions to put to an employer before accepting:

- Which status of residence will you sponsor, and what period of stay do you expect to be granted?
- Who pays the immigration fees, now that they are material?
- What are salary, hours, paid leave, relocation support, insurance and pension enrolment, in writing?
- Who helps with Japanese-language paperwork, and when does that help end?
- What happens to the status if the job ends?

**Why the category matters for permanent residence.** A revised permanent-residence guideline was issued on 1 October
2026. It applies in stages: applications filed from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 are judged under the February 2026
guideline plus the new income and public-burden sections only; applications from 1 April 2027 are judged under the full
new text. **V** [ISA-pr-transition] [ISA-pr-guideline]

What the new guideline weighs. These are stated as factors considered, not automatic thresholds:

| Factor | What it says | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese | About B1 level or above. Exempt: 70- and 80-point highly skilled professionals and their families, people with six or more years of Japanese primary or secondary schooling, and some Japan-born children of permanent residents. | **V** |
| Income | Household income continuously above the average Japanese household income for a household of that size. No yen figure is published. A dependent spouse's part-time income is excluded. | **V** |
| Pension | A projection, not years already paid: the estimated future benefit should reach what someone earning that benchmark for 30 years on Employees' Pension would receive. A shortfall can be covered by financial assets scaled to age. | **V** |
| Period of stay | From 1 April 2027 a three-year status no longer counts as the "longest period of stay", with narrow grandfathering. New arrivals aiming at permanent residence will need a five-year status. | **V** |
| Absence | One absence of six months or more, or 2.5 cumulative years abroad in ten, weighs against the applicant. | **V** |
| Payments | Late tax, pension or health-insurance payments count against the applicant even when later paid. | **V** |
| Spouse route | Five years of marriage plus three of residence, up from three plus one. | **V** |
| Fast routes | The 80-point (one year) and 70-point (three years) highly skilled professional routes are retained. | **V** |

One point needs a lawyer before the visa category is chosen. On one reading of the new guideline's notes,
time spent as an Intra-company Transferee (企業内転勤) no longer counts as "work status" time for applications from
1 April 2027. That is an interpretation of the Japanese text made during this review, not a published ISA statement.
**Unconfirmed.** If permanent residence is a possible goal, ask the question directly.

permission: two adults renewing for three years pay ¥128,000 at the counter or ¥113,100 online. Reductions exist only
for people near public-assistance level who also meet a humanitarian criterion. These are not entry-visa fees.

### 3.2 Money in two countries

Neither input report treated this properly. It is where the largest avoidable costs sit, and almost all of it turns on
facts an adviser must confirm for the household. Take the following as the agenda for that conversation.

**United States side**

| Point | What the source says | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Worldwide taxation | US citizens abroad are taxed on worldwide income and must file. The US–Japan tax treaty's saving clause lets the US tax its citizens regardless of the treaty. Filers abroad get an automatic extension to 15 June. | **V** [IRS-abroad] [IRS-treaty] |
| Foreign Earned Income Exclusion | $132,900 for tax year 2026; $137,300 for tax year 2027. It is elected on a filed return, not automatic. The alternative is the foreign tax credit; which is better depends on Japanese tax actually paid. | **V** [IRS-2027] |
| FBAR | Required when foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any time in the year. | **V** [IRS-8938-FBAR] |
| Form 8938 | For joint filers living abroad: over $400,000 at year end or $600,000 at any time. Single: $200,000 or $300,000. | **V** [IRS-8938-FBAR] |
| Japanese funds | A fund is a PFIC if 75% of income or 50% of assets is passive. Only US plans are carved out. Japanese-domiciled mutual funds and ETFs, including inside NISA, therefore generally fall in. The IRS text does not name NISA; this is an inference, and a safe one. | **V** rule, inferred application [IRS-8621] |

**Japan side**

| Point | What the source says | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Non-permanent resident | A non-Japanese national who has had a domicile or residence in Japan for five years or less in the preceding ten. Taxed on (a) income that is not foreign-source, (b) foreign-source income paid in Japan, and (c) foreign-source income paid abroad and remitted to Japan. After that, on all income worldwide. | **V** [NTA-residency] |
| Residence tax | Charged by the municipality where the address is registered on 1 January, on the previous calendar year's income: 10% (4% metropolitan, 6% ward) plus ¥5,000 per person. Leaving after 1 January does not cancel that year's bill. It follows that an arrival after 1 January brings no residence tax bill for that fiscal year; no page states this outright, so confirm with the ward. | **V** rule, inferred consequence [TMG-resident-tax] |
| Exit tax | Applies on leaving Japan to residents with ¥100 million or more of covered financial assets who were resident for more than five of the previous ten years. Time under a Table 1 status of residence (the work-type statuses) does not count toward the five years. | **V** [NTA-exit] |
| Inheritance tax | A "temporary resident" (Table 1 status, domiciled in Japan ten years or less in the past fifteen) is in scope for Japan-situs assets only, provided the person who died was also a foreign or non-resident decedent. Otherwise scope is worldwide. A US–Japan estate and gift tax treaty exists. | **V** [NTA-inherit] [IRS-treaties] |

Three consequences worth stating plainly:

- **The remittance rule rewards keeping foreign income offshore for five years.** Money moved to Japan in a year when
  foreign-source income was earned can be treated as a remittance of that income. Plan living costs around
  Japan-source salary and pre-arrival savings, and get the mechanics from the adviser before the first transfer.
  Gains on some securities are treated as non-foreign-source for this purpose; that detail was not checked in this
  review and belongs on the adviser's list.
- **Both shelters above depend on holding a Table 1 status.** A later change to spouse-of-Japanese or permanent
  resident status ends them. That makes permanent residence a tax decision as well as an immigration one.
- **Do not buy Japanese funds, in NISA or outside it,** without specific US tax advice.

**Social security**

- The US–Japan agreement lets a worker sent temporarily by a US employer stay in the US system for up to five years,
  extendable, with a certificate of coverage. **V** [JPS-US]
- Keeping US Social Security does not by itself remove Japanese health insurance. The health-insurance exemption
  applies only if the worker, spouse and children all hold private insurance covering care in Japan. One uninsured
  family member voids it. **V** [JPS-US]
- The Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment for foreigners who leave is capped at 60 months of contributions. Taking it makes all
  earlier Japanese periods invalid for totalization. A rise to eight years is planned but not in force, and a 2025 law
  will bar claims while a re-entry permit is valid, from a date still to be set. Do not plan around the refund. **V**
  [JPS-lumpsum] / **S** for the eight-year plan

### 3.3 Housing

**Choose around the actual week.** Start with the commute, the budget and daily routines, and compare individual
properties and stations rather than ranking wards by how "authentically Japanese" they seem. An internationally
oriented neighbourhood gives support; a Japanese-speaking one gives practice. Neither creates friendships by itself.
**J**

| Decision | What to check before signing |
|---|---|
| Commute | Door-to-door time at the real travel hour, transfers, walking distance to the station, the last train home. |
| Total cost | Rent, common charges, deposit, key money, agent and guarantor fees, insurance, renewal fee, move-out terms. |
| Building | Noise transmission, ventilation, heating and cooling, internet options, storage, permission for pets and home working. |
| The dog | Written pet permission, size and breed limits, extra deposit, where the dog may be walked through shared areas, the nearest vet. |
| Safety | The ward's flood and hazard maps, evacuation site, whether furniture can be fixed to walls. |
| Contract | Permitted occupants, notice period, early-termination charge, cleaning charge, whether a fixed-term lease can continue. |

**What it costs to move in.** The transport ministry's multilingual *Apartment Search Guidebook* (14 languages) puts
initial costs at the equivalent of four to seven months' rent: deposit about two months, key money one to two months
in Kanto, agency fee up to one month, and a guarantee-company fee of about half a month, or 35–50% of a month's rent,
covering two years. The original report's "three to five months" understated it. Get an itemised quotation before
treating any multiple as a budget, and keep a separate allowance for a bridge stay, furnishings and cash. **V**
[MLIT-guide]

**The dog narrows the field.** On one national portal, 19.3% of rental listings accepted pets in March 2025, up from
12.9% in 2021. Pet-friendly listings carried a higher average rent (¥112,771 against ¥78,253) and were taken faster
(66.8 days listed against 83.4). An older Tokyo figure from the same portal was 16.4% in October 2022. No portal
dataset was found for the often-quoted "one extra month of deposit"; it varies by landlord. **S** [LIFULL-pets]

**UR public rental housing** is worth a look precisely because it removes the screening fees: no key money, no
brokerage fee, no renewal fee and no guarantor. The deposit is two months' rent. Income requirements apply (monthly
household income of four times the rent below ¥82,500; ¥330,000 for rents from ¥82,500 to under ¥200,000; ¥400,000
above that), with alternatives of prepaying one to ten years or holding savings of 100 times the rent. Mid-to-long-term
residents are eligible. Income evidence is Japan-based, and under a year of employment needs UR's own income
certificate. Pet rules vary by estate; ask. **V** [UR]

**Refusals happen, and they are not simply "legal".** Japan has no national statute expressly prohibiting
nationality-based refusal in private rental housing. Courts have nonetheless awarded damages for such refusals, so the
original's flat statement that refusal "is legal" was wrong; the accurate position is that the remedy is litigation
after the fact. **V** [MOJ-rights]

How common it is, with the denominators kept visible:

| Figure | What it measures | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 19.4% | Of all 8,874 respondents to the FY2025 national survey of foreign residents, the share reporting discrimination while house-hunting. It was the most common setting. (17.4% in FY2024.) | **V** [ISA-survey-2025] |
| 23.8% | Of the 3,902 who reported any discrimination in FY2025, the share naming housing or real-estate staff. (25.1% in FY2024. The original's 27.6% was a misread chart.) | **V** [ISA-survey-2025] [ISA-survey-2024] |

experienced with foreign tenants, prepare a short Japanese-language introduction covering employer, period of stay and

### 3.4 Medicines

Check each medicine by active ingredient. **V** [NCD] [MHLW-import]

| Medicine | Position | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Amphetamine and methamphetamine products (Adderall, Dexedrine) | Cannot be imported, "even for treatment purposes". A US prescription gives no protection. | Work with the prescriber on a different treatment well before the move. |
| Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse) | A stimulant raw material. Allowed only with advance permission from the Narcotics Control Department. | Apply at least 14 days before arrival. Plan on the email route. |
| Methylphenidate (Concerta) | A psychotropic. No permission needed up to 2.16 g in total, non-injection. Above that, carry a doctor's certificate. For more than one month's supply the official page says to contact the import-confirmation office. | Count the total milligrams being carried. |
| Other psychotropics | Permission-free up to set totals: zolpidem 300 mg, alprazolam 72 mg, diazepam 1.2 g, lorazepam 90 mg, clonazepam 180 mg, modafinil 6 g. | Check each one against the official table. |
| Narcotics (many opioid painkillers) | Permission required. Classification is by ingredient, so "opioids" as a class is too loose. | Check the ingredient, then apply. |
| Ordinary prescription medicines | Up to one month's supply needs no procedure. More needs an Import Confirmation, applied for online and issued in a few business days. | Apply for anything over a month. |
| Atomoxetine (Strattera) | Not mentioned on the NCD page. The ordinary one-month rule presumably applies. **Not verified.** | Confirm before relying on it. |

Further points:

- Narcotics, psychotropics and stimulant raw materials may not be mailed, and should not be carried by someone else.
- An online application trial exists, but it is narrow: stimulant raw materials only, direct arrival at Haneda or
  Narita, documents sent at least 14 business days before entry, and entry on or before 20 December. The page prints
  no year. Treat it as possibly gone by the time of the move. The earlier review described it as a general
  "November 2026 trial"; that overstated it.
- Year-end and other holiday periods have earlier cut-off dates. Check the notice for the travel date.
- **Cannabis and CBD.** Use of cannabis has been an offence since 12 December 2024, with up to seven years'
  imprisonment for possession or use. THC residue limits for CBD products have applied from the same date: 10 ppm for
  oils, 0.1 ppm for water-based drinks, 1 ppm for other products. The NCD's CBD page also says CBN was made a
  designated substance from 1 June 2026, which catches many US sleep products. Leave all of it behind. **V**
  [NPA-cannabis] [NCD]
- **A permit covers one entry.** Leaving Japan with the medicine and coming back needs an export permit and a new
  import permit each time. It cannot be mailed, and nobody else may carry it, family included. **V** [NCD]
- **Continuing treatment after arrival is a separate problem from getting the first supply in.** Vyvanse is sold in
  Japan, but its label covers childhood ADHD: it says efficacy and safety are not established at 18 and over, allows
  patients who started as children to continue, and requires prescriber, pharmacy and patient to be enrolled in a
  distribution registry. Concerta is labelled for adults under the same registry, but has been on limited shipment
  since September 2025, and in June 2026 its maker asked prescribers not to start new patients. Atomoxetine and
  guanfacine are labelled for adults with no registry. Which of these suits a person is a medical decision; the
  planning point is to settle it with the prescriber months before the move. **V** [PMDA-vyvanse] [MHLW-concerta]
- Line up a Japanese clinic for refills before the supply brought from the US runs out. **J**

### 3.6 The dog

Neither input report mentioned pets. For this household it is the longest-lead item in the move. **V** [AQS-dog]
[AQS-designated] [APHIS]

The sequence for a dog coming from the continental United States:

1. **Microchip** to ISO 11784/11785, implanted before the first rabies vaccination that is to count. A vaccination
   given before the chip does not count.
2. **First rabies vaccination** at 91 days of age or older, with an inactivated or recombinant vaccine.
3. **Second rabies vaccination** 30 days or more after the first and within the first one's validity.
4. **Antibody test** on blood drawn after the second vaccination (the same day is allowed), at a designated
   laboratory. In the US these are Kansas State University and the Department of Defense laboratory. The result must
   be 0.5 IU/ml or higher.
5. **Wait 180 days** from the blood-sampling date, counted with sampling day as day zero. The result stays valid for
   two years, provided vaccinations are kept current.
6. **Advance notification** to the Animal Quarantine Service not less than 40 days before arrival, through the NACCS
   online system. The USDA page still says "by fax or mail"; the Japanese side is the authority and has moved on.
7. **Clinical examination** by an accredited vet within 10 days before boarding, and **USDA APHIS endorsement** of the
   certificate. Electronic certificates from the US have been accepted since 26 March 2024.
8. **Arrival** at one of 11 designated airports, which include Narita and Haneda. With everything in order the
   inspection takes up to 12 hours. Without it, quarantine can last up to 180 days.

Things that catch people out:

- A dog with existing rabies vaccinations may still be at step one if the chip was implanted afterwards or is not an
  ISO chip. Check the dates on the records before assuming a shorter path.
- A December 2025 notice warns that some microchip number prefixes are duplicated across manufacturers and may force
  re-chipping and a restart. Check the chip number against that notice early.
- Fix the flight and the airport before filing the notification. The arrival date can be moved later, not earlier.
- Hawaii and Guam are "designated regions": a dog resident there for 180 days needs no rabies vaccination, antibody
  test or wait. The chip, the 40-day notification and the examination within 10 days still apply, and the flight
  must not pass through a non-designated region such as the mainland. None of this applies to a mainland dog.
- For a dog travelling as accompanied baggage the quarantine service says it "is available anytime"; fixed
  reception hours apply to cargo. If someone other than the owner presents the dog, family included, a power of
  attorney is needed. [AQS-qa]
- Pet food containing meat cannot be brought in. A prescription diet has to be sourced in Japan. [AQS-qa]
- "What if the dog arrives later?" is a legitimate option when the 180 days cannot be met: the dog waits it out in the
  US and follows with a second trip or a pet shipper, rather than waiting in quarantine.

After arrival in Tokyo: register the dog within 30 days using the import quarantine certificate. In wards that have
adopted the microchip scheme, Minato among them, entering the chip in the Environment Ministry's database is the
registration and no ward visit is needed (¥400 online) [Minato-dog];
vaccinate against rabies once a year between 1 April and 30 June; keep the tags on the dog. The Tokyo ordinance
requires a dog to be tethered or on a lead, a bite to be reported within 24 hours, and the dog to be checked by a vet
for rabies within 48 hours. **V** [TMG-dog]

### 3.7 Language

Aim at tasks, not exam levels: give an address, understand a price, make an appointment, report a problem, check an

A sequence that works, restored from the original report with its overclaims removed. **J**

## 5. Rules that affect other people

In the 2023 national survey of 4,424 Japanese citizens, 77.5% said they want foreign residents to observe Japanese
customs and living rules, and 58.6% named more interaction between Japanese and foreign residents as the top measure
needed. Rule-keeping is the expectation that the evidence actually supports. **V** [ISA-citizens-2023]

### At home

- **Noise.** Japanese apartment buildings often carry sound readily. There is no nationwide quiet-hours rule; the
  original's "10 p.m. to 8 a.m." is a common building convention, not law. Ask the manager about washing machines,
  vacuuming and the dog. **J**
- **Shared space.** Balconies and corridors are frequently escape routes. Keep them clear, and ask before smoking or
  cooking outside. **J**
- **The dog.** Barking is the noise complaint most likely to reach the manager. Know the lead rule above, carry
  water to rinse where the dog urinates outdoors, and take waste home; these are common expectations in Tokyo
  neighbourhoods. **J**

**How complaints travel.** One company survey (542 respondents with moving experience, not Tokyo-specific and not
about foreigners) found 62.2% had experienced trouble with a neighbour. Of those, 36.9% went to the management company
or municipality, 30.3% did nothing, 20.2% spoke to the neighbour directly and 6.0% called the police. The original
report claimed "the majority escalated to third parties"; they did not. The safer reading: a problem may be reported
to the manager, or silently tolerated, more often than raised at the door, so the absence of complaints is weak
evidence that all is well. **V** [FLIE]

A second survey often quoted for "noise 71.4%, rubbish 33.0%" asked 500 renters what they *think* tends to cause
trouble with foreign tenants. It measures perception, not incidents. **V** [AlbaLink]

### Rubbish

### Moving-in greetings

Ask the manager what is normal in the building. The survey evidence splits by housing type: about 80% of 1,042 home
buyers greeted neighbours (fieldwork November 2024 to January 2025, not "July 2026" as the original said), while 73.8%
of working singles in one-room flats in the Tokyo metropolitan area called greetings unnecessary. A brief
introduction to adjacent neighbours is reasonable in a family building or before noisy move-in work, and a dog is a
good reason to make one. It is not compulsory. **V** [ZeroRenove] / **S** [FJNext]

### Trains and shared space

- Phones on silent and no voice calls in the carriage; let passengers off first; keep bags out of the way. Tokyo
  Metro asks for exactly this. Women-only cars run on weekday mornings, with hours that vary by line. **S**
  [TokyoMetro-faq]
- **Escalators.** The original said to stand on the left. The current guidance from 58 rail operators, including
  Tokyo Metro and JR East, is to stand still and hold the handrail, and it calls leaving one side open for walkers
  dangerous. Local habit still leaves a side open; the official line does not. **V** [Escalator]
- **Restaurants and shops.** No tipping; place money on the tray at the till; a small cover charge with a starter is
  normal at an izakaya; do not stand chopsticks upright in rice or pass food from chopsticks to chopsticks. **J**

### Cycling

Ride on the left, obey signals and stop signs, use a light at night. Liability insurance has been required by Tokyo
ordinance since 1 April 2020. Helmets have been a duty to make an effort, without a penalty, since 1 April 2023. **V**
[TMPD-insurance] [NPA-helmet]

Since 1 April 2026 riders aged 16 and over can be given a "blue ticket" for specified violations. **V**
[NPA-rulebook] [NPA-system]

| Fine | Violations |
|---|---|
| ¥12,000 | Holding a phone to call, or staring at its screen, while riding |
| ¥7,000 | Entering a closed railway crossing |
| ¥6,000 | Running a red light; riding on the wrong side; riding on a pavement where it is not permitted |
| ¥5,000 | Holding an umbrella; earphones that block sound; no light at night; ignoring a stop sign; defective brakes |

How it works in practice:

- Guidance and a warning remain the default. Tickets are for dangerous or wilful violations. Ordinary pavement
  riding normally draws a warning only. One-ear or open-ear earphones are not a violation if traffic can be heard.
- Police never collect money at the roadside. Payment is due in principle within seven days from the day after the

  moves to criminal procedure.
- **Riding after drinking is a criminal offence,** outside the ticket system: up to five years or ¥1,000,000 when
  intoxicated, and up to three years or ¥500,000 at 0.15 mg/l of breath alcohol or more.
- **Immigration effect.** The original said a paid blue ticket goes on a record that can affect visa renewal or
  permanent residence. No official statement supports that. The police say a paid ticket brings no prosecution and
  no criminal record. The permanent-residence guideline looks at criminal fines and overall good conduct, so an
  unpaid ticket that turns into a criminal fine, or drunk cycling, plausibly would matter. That last step is an
  inference. Pay inside the window.

## 6. Health and emergencies

**Start at a clinic.** Find a nearby clinic and pharmacy, with hours, booking method and language support, before
they are needed. Going straight to a large hospital without a referral letter costs extra: at least ¥7,000 for a
first medical visit at special-function hospitals and at regional-support and referral-focused hospitals with 200 or
more general beds. This never applies to an emergency. Prescriptions are valid for four days including the day of
issue and holidays. **V** [MHLW-referral]

**The monthly ceiling on medical costs** is real but is not a flat figure. For the middle-income band (roughly ¥3.7 to
¥7.7 million a year), under 70: **V** [Kyokai-highcost]

| Period | Monthly ceiling |
|---|---|
| To July 2026 | ¥80,100 + 1% of covered costs above ¥267,000 |
| From August 2026 | ¥85,800 + 1% of covered costs above ¥286,000. Fourth and later months in a year: ¥44,400. A new annual cap of ¥530,000. |
| From August 2027 | The band splits. About ¥3.7–5.1 million: unchanged. About ¥5.1–6.5 million: ¥98,100 + 1% above ¥327,000. About ¥6.5–7.7 million: ¥110,400 + 1% above ¥368,000. |

At ¥1,000,000 of covered care in one month today, the ceiling works out at ¥92,940. Showing a My Number insurance card
applies the ceiling at the counter of a participating provider; costs across several providers may still need a
reimbursement claim, and uncovered charges such as private rooms sit outside it.

**Prepare for an earthquake as a routine, not a project.** Check the ward's hazard maps for the address, learn the
building's evacuation arrangements, fix heavy furniture, and agree where the household meets. Tokyo's own guidance is
to stockpile at least three days of supplies first and plan toward a week or more: water, food, light, charging,
medicines and emergency toilets. Add the dog: food, water, a crate, a spare lead, vaccination records, and a check on
whether the local evacuation site admits animals. Join a neighbourhood drill when one is offered; it is also the
easiest way to meet the neighbours. **V** [TMG-stockpile] / **J** for the dog items

## 7. Work

Treat every cultural framework as a hypothesis to test against the actual team. Domestic firms, foreign-affiliated
firms, startups and individual managers differ widely.

**First-week questions.** **J**

- Who makes the final decision, and who needs to be consulted first?
- When do you want to see an outline, a draft, a finished piece?
- How often do you want updates, and what counts as urgent?
- How should I confirm a decision and next steps?
- What are the real expectations on hours, remote work, leave, dress and social events?

**Three working habits worth adopting early.** **J**, with sources as noted.

1. **Share early, share often.** Consultant Rochelle Kopp describes work being sent back "as many as ten or fifteen
   times" when a manager was not consulted along the way. Bring an outline before a finished product, and report bad
   news as soon as it exists. [Kopp-horenso]
2. **Consult before the meeting.** Proposals often go better when the people affected have seen them individually
   first, and formal meetings may confirm rather than decide. The original's "two to six weeks ahead" and "eight to
   twelve approvals" are invented precision; ask how it works here.
3. **Lead with the apology, then the fix.** Acknowledge the effect on others, explain the correction, agree how to
   prevent a repeat. Opening with reasons can read as excuse-making. [Kopp-excuses]

Raise urgent safety, legal or delivery problems promptly through the right channel. Preserving harmony is never a
reason to conceal them.

**Indirect wording needs a clarifying question, not a codebook.** The original presented each phrase below as having
one hidden meaning. Each has several.

| What you hear | It may mean | A useful response |
|---|---|---|
| "It may be a little difficult." | A real constraint, or a polite no. | "Which constraint should we plan around?" |
| "We will consider it." | Genuine review, open questions, or a decline. | "What would help, and when should we come back to it?" |
| "We need to discuss internally." | Others must agree; there may be concerns. | "Who needs to see it, and what can I help answer?" |
| A nod, or "hai" | "I am following", not necessarily "I agree". | "To confirm: are we going ahead, or is more review needed?" |
| A long pause | Thinking, composing a reply in a second language, or discomfort. | Wait. Then offer to continue one to one. |

**What the cited authorities actually support.**

- Erin Meyer describes Japan as very hierarchical and at the same time consensual in decision-making. The claim that
  the US is the lowest-context culture in the world and Japan the highest comes from summaries of her book and was
  not checked against it. Her framework describes relative national averages, not colleagues. **V** / **S**
  [Meyer-INSEAD]
- Kopp bans the terms *honne* and *tatemae* from her seminars because teaching them "makes it sound like Japanese
  have a unique propensity to be liars". Politeness and private reservations exist everywhere. **V** [Kopp-honne]
- The article advising against irony was written by Patricia Pringle, not Kopp as the original said. The advice
  holds: sarcasm travels badly into a second language. **V** [Pringle-irony]

**Conventions to observe rather than memorise.** Business cards offered with both hands, family name plus *-san*,
waiting to be shown a seat, a box of individually wrapped sweets for the team after a trip. Watch what colleagues do
and ask. Bow angles and conversational distances given to the degree and the foot in the original were not
supportable. **J**

**Dress.** Tokyo's governor announced on 3 April 2026 that the metropolitan government would work without ties or
jackets, in polo shirts, T-shirts and trainers, "and shorts depending on the work", and asked residents and
businesses to follow. It does not set any private employer's dress code. Dress conservatively in week one and match
the team. **V** [TMG-coolbiz]

**After-work drinking.** The original promised a "generational paradox" and never stated it. It is this: workers in
their twenties are the keenest on year-end parties (73.6% want to attend, against 60.0% of those in their fifties,
in a 2025 survey of 362 Tokyo workers), yet 56.4% of workers overall, and 55.7% of those in their twenties, say
drinking-party communication is unnecessary (2024, n = 11,377). In a 2023 survey of 100,000 workers, about 73% of men
and 86% of women said being scolded for skipping a party amounts to harassment. In short: attendance is optional,
alcohol is optional, and turning up occasionally to a welcome or year-end party is still appreciated. **S** [Nonpi]
[NipponLife] [Persol]

**Foreign-affiliated firms.** The last government survey of them covered fiscal 2019: 2,808 firms and roughly 400,000
to 510,000 employees depending on the basis. It has since been discontinued, so the figure is old, and the "0.6% of
the workforce" in the original is not from that survey. The point it was used for stands as judgment: such firms hire
largely from Japanese companies and serve Japanese clients, so do not assume a foreign name means a foreign working
culture. The claim that they pay 10–20% more could not be verified. **V** for the count [METI] / **J**

## 8. Community

**What the evidence says.** In the FY2025 national survey of foreign residents (8,874 respondents), 91.0% were
satisfied or somewhat satisfied with life in Japan, 19.8% felt lonely sometimes and 5.7% often or always. All three
improved on FY2024 (88.2%, 24.7%, 7.3%), so the original's picture of rising loneliness is out of date. Only 0.4% in
FY2024 and 0.2% in FY2025 said they could not join activities because they were foreign; the original's 1.7% was a
misread chart, and the true figure makes its point more strongly. These are national figures for all foreign
residents, not a forecast for Americans in Tokyo. **V** [ISA-survey-2025] [ISA-survey-2024]

**Four channels that long-term residents report working.** All **J**; none is a guarantee.

1. **An activity pursued in Japanese, every week.** A sport, music, craft, game or class chosen because it is
   enjoyable enough to repeat. Regular attendance over months is what turns polite distance into familiarity.
2. **Being a regular somewhere small.** A counter restaurant, a coffee shop or a standing bar near the station, on
   the same evening each week.
3. **The ward's own structures.** The international association, the volunteer centre run by the social welfare
   council, and volunteer-run Japanese classes. See the resources in [3.7](#37-language).
4. **The neighbourhood association and its festival.** Associations run disaster drills, clean-ups and the local
   festival. Joining procedures differ everywhere; ask the building manager or the ward office.

A fifth, for this household: **the dog.** The same walk at the same time each day produces more repeated, low-stakes
contact with neighbours than any class. **J**

**Pacing.** **J**

- Make specific invitations with a date and a place, and expect diaries to be fuller further ahead than in the US.
  The original's "two to four weeks" is a guess.
- Friendships are commonly conducted in restaurants and cafés rather than at home. Not being invited round is not a
  verdict.
- LINE is the usual messaging app. Agree expectations rather than watching read receipts.
- For gifts, something modest and consumable is right when visiting or returning from a trip. Accept a refusal. No
  fixed-value repayment formula is required for everyday friendship.
- Keep friendships in English too. A support network does not make local participation less real, and the
  nationality mix of one's friends is a poor measure of progress.

**Ambiguous moments.** A compliment on one's Japanese may be friendly, habitual or patronising. An empty seat on a
train does not reveal the stranger's reason. There is no need to force either a generous or a hostile reading onto
every such encounter. Save the energy for concrete patterns that affect rights, work, housing or access to services,
and use the consultation line above when they arise.

What to do with this: nothing dramatic. The survey of Japanese citizens points the same way as the everyday advice.

**If permanent residence might become a goal,** the habits that keep it open start on arrival: pay tax, pension and

**Expect the experience to change.** The four-stage culture-shock story (honeymoon, frustration, adjustment,
adaptation) is a loose description, not a schedule. Operating in a second language while monitoring one's effect on
others is tiring. Two failure modes are worth naming: using foreignness as a licence to ignore inconvenient rules,
and the opposite, grinding oneself down to prove how well one fits. Respect the rules that protect shared space, and
decline unnecessary self-erasure. As one long-term American resident put it: "I can adapt without disappearing. I can
respect Japanese culture without pretending that I never see things differently." [Jubelt]
